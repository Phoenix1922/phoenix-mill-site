Vendor Checklist for a Smooth Wedding Setup

Arrival timing, load-in, setup, coordination, and venue etiquette.

A beautiful wedding doesn't appear all at once.

Before the first guest arrives, there's usually a small army at work: tables are being dressed, flowers are being arranged, food is being prepared, speakers are being tested, photographers are scouting locations, desserts are being delivered, and dozens of little details are finding their places.

The secret to a smooth setup isn't necessarily having fewer vendors.

It's making sure everyone knows when to arrive, where to go, what they're responsible for, and when they need to be finished.

Use this checklist as you coordinate your wedding at Phoenix Mill Events. Fill in the blanks as you confirm details with each vendor, then share the completed information with your planner or day-of point person.

Your Vendor-by-Vendor Setup Checklist

For every vendor, answer the same basic questions:

Who are they? When are they arriving? Where are they going? What are they bringing? How long do they need to set up? Who is their contact person? And what happens to their things at the end of the night?

Establish Your Setup Order

Some vendors can't do their jobs until someone else finishes theirs.

For example, linens can't go onto tables until the tables are positioned. Place settings can't be completed until linens are down. Florists may need tables ready before placing centerpieces. DJs may need access before décor makes equipment areas difficult to reach.

Write down the order that makes sense for your event:

1. _________________________________________________

2. _________________________________________________

3. _________________________________________________

4. _________________________________________________

5. _________________________________________________

6. _________________________________________________

Then ask yourself:

Are any of these vendors scheduled to arrive before the thing they need is ready?

That simple question can catch a lot of scheduling problems.

Venue Etiquette: Help Everyone Leave the Space as They Found It

Your vendors should understand that they're working inside a venue—not simply an empty room.

Before the event, make sure everyone knows the rules regarding:

☐ Hanging or attaching decorations
☐ Candles / open flame
☐ Confetti, glitter, or similar materials
☐ Electrical equipment
☐ Furniture movement
☐ Alcohol service
☐ Kitchen / service areas
☐ Trash removal
☐ Vendor parking
☐ Smoking
☐ End-of-night cleanup
☐ Items left overnight
☐ Rental pickup
☐ Other venue-specific requirements

This is especially important in a historic property. Historic character is part of what makes Phoenix Mill special, and decorations and equipment should be installed in ways that respect and protect the building.

If you're unsure whether something can be attached, moved, plugged in, installed, or brought into the venue, ask before the wedding day.

The 30-Minute Test

Here's one final exercise.

Imagine it is 30 minutes before guests arrive.

Walk through the wedding in your head.

☐ Is the ceremony area finished?

☐ Are the tables completely set?

☐ Are flowers in place?

☐ Is the bar ready?

☐ Is the DJ finished testing sound?

☐ Is the cake safely where it belongs?

☐ Are signs and place cards out?

☐ Are caterers prepared?

☐ Are vendor boxes, carts, and packing materials out of guest areas?

☐ Is everyone dressed who needs to be?

☐ Is there anyone who still needs the bride or groom to answer a question?

If the answer to that last question is no, you've done your job.

A Smooth Setup Makes for a Better Celebration

The point of all these checklists isn't to make your wedding feel complicated.

It's exactly the opposite.

When arrival times are established, responsibilities are clear, vendors understand the venue, and everyone knows whom to contact, the work happens quietly behind the scenes.

By the time your guests arrive at Phoenix Mill, they shouldn't see the setup.

They should simply see your wedding.
