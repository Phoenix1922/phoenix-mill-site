PLANNING GUIDE

Vendor Checklist for a Smooth Wedding Setup

Arrival timing, load-in, setup responsibilities, and the details that help your wedding day come together smoothly.

On your wedding day, a surprising number of people may arrive at the venue before the first guest ever walks through the door.

Caterers. Florists. DJs. Photographers. Bakers. Bartenders. Rental companies. Decorators. Musicians. Photo-booth companies. Planners.

Each vendor may have a completely different job, but they all share one thing:

They need to know where to go, when to arrive, what they're responsible for, and when everything needs to be ready.

That's why good vendor coordination can make such a difference in how smoothly a wedding setup unfolds.

You don't need to personally manage every vendor on your wedding day. In fact, you shouldn't.

But before the day arrives, making sure everyone has the right information can prevent a lot of unnecessary questions, delays, and confusion.

Here's a practical checklist to help.

Start With One Complete Vendor List

Begin by putting every wedding vendor in one place.

Don't rely on contracts scattered across your email, phone numbers buried in text messages, or the assumption that someone else has the information.

Your master vendor list should include:

  • Company name
  • Primary contact person
  • Cell phone number
  • Email address
  • Service being provided
  • Scheduled arrival time
  • Expected setup time
  • Service start time
  • Breakdown or pickup time

Include every company that will physically arrive at the venue, even if they'll only be there for a few minutes.

That means the bakery delivering the cake belongs on the list just as much as the DJ who will be there all evening.

Confirm Arrival Times Before the Wedding

"We'll be there sometime in the afternoon" isn't much of a setup plan.

As your wedding gets closer, confirm an expected arrival time with every vendor who needs access to the venue.

Then compare those times with the rest of your setup schedule.

For example, if your florist needs tables in their final positions before placing centerpieces, the tables need to be ready before the florist begins.

If the DJ needs access to a particular area, you don't want rental equipment or decorating supplies blocking it when the DJ arrives.

If the caterer needs to begin food preparation several hours before dinner, their arrival time should reflect that.

Think of setup as a sequence rather than a collection of unrelated arrival times.

Don't Schedule Everyone to Arrive at Once

If venue access begins at a certain time, it's tempting to tell every vendor to arrive at that exact moment.

That can create unnecessary congestion.

Suddenly several vehicles are unloading at the same time, vendors are trying to use the same doors, equipment is sitting in walkways, and everyone is asking where they should go.

When possible, stagger vendor arrivals based on what actually needs to happen first.

A typical sequence might look something like:

  • Major rentals and equipment
  • Tables and room setup
  • Florals and décor
  • DJ, band, and entertainment equipment
  • Catering and bar setup
  • Cake and dessert delivery
  • Photography and videography
  • Final detail items

Your actual order will depend on your wedding, but the principle is simple:

Give people enough room and time to do their jobs.

Make Sure Vendors Know Where to Go

A vendor arriving at a venue for the first time shouldn't have to guess where to unload.

Before the wedding, make sure each vendor knows the basic logistics that apply to them.

That may include:

  • The venue address
  • Which entrance to use
  • Where loading and unloading should occur
  • Where vehicles should be moved after unloading
  • Where their equipment or service area will be located
  • Who they should contact when they arrive

This is especially important for vendors bringing large or heavy equipment.

Rental companies, DJs, bands, caterers, photo booths, and decorators may arrive with considerably more than they can carry through the door in one trip.

The clearer the load-in plan is beforehand, the easier setup becomes.

Tell Vendors Where Their Setup Belongs

Knowing that there will be a DJ booth, cake table, buffet, photo booth, and bar isn't enough.

Someone should know where each of those things actually goes.

A basic floor plan can be extremely helpful.

It doesn't need to be an architectural drawing. It simply needs to communicate the important locations clearly.

Consider identifying:

  • Guest tables
  • Head table or sweetheart table
  • DJ or band location
  • Dance floor
  • Bar
  • Buffet or food-service area
  • Cake or dessert table
  • Gift and card table
  • Guest book
  • Photo booth
  • Seating chart or escort-card display
  • Special décor or installations

If a vendor needs a particular location, don't leave that decision until they're standing in the room with equipment in their hands.

Identify Power and Equipment Needs Ahead of Time

Some vendors need considerably more than an empty space.

DJs, bands, photo booths, caterers, specialty lighting companies, and other vendors may require electrical power or other accommodations.

Ask ahead of time what they're bringing and what they need.

Questions might include:

  • Do you need electrical power?
  • How many outlets do you need?
  • Are there special electrical requirements?
  • How much equipment are you bringing?
  • How much setup space do you need?
  • Do you need tables provided?
  • Do you need access to water?
  • Do you need refrigeration or food-service space?

A five-minute conversation before the wedding is much easier than discovering a problem during setup.

Be Clear About Who Is Providing What

Some of the most frustrating wedding-day problems aren't caused by anything going wrong.

They're caused by two people assuming the other person was responsible for something.

Who is providing the cake table?

Who brings linens?

Who supplies serving equipment?

Who provides extension cords?

Who lights the candles?

Who puts the place cards on the tables?

Who moves ceremony flowers to the reception?

Who removes décor at the end of the night?

None of these questions is particularly complicated.

But if nobody answers them until the wedding day, they can become surprisingly disruptive.

For anything important, assign responsibility to a specific person or vendor.

Think About the Order of Setup

Wedding setup is much easier when people aren't repeatedly undoing someone else's work.

For example, delicate centerpieces probably shouldn't be placed before tables are in their final locations.

Place settings shouldn't be arranged while ladders and large decorating equipment are still moving through the room.

A cake shouldn't be delivered to a table that hasn't been positioned or dressed yet.

And final decorative details shouldn't prevent a vendor from accessing something they still need to install.

A useful way to think about setup is:

Big things first. Fragile things later. Final details last.

Establish a Setup Deadline

Vendor arrival time and vendor completion time are two different things.

If guests may begin arriving at 4:30, the goal shouldn't be for setup to finish at 4:30.

Give yourself some separation between active setup and guest arrival whenever possible.

That buffer allows time to:

  • Remove boxes and packaging
  • Move vendor vehicles
  • Clean up setup debris
  • Make last-minute adjustments
  • Check tables and décor
  • Complete sound checks
  • Allow photographers to capture room details

It also gives the room a chance to stop looking like a workspace and start looking like a wedding.

Give Your Photographer a Finished Room

If photographs of your reception setup are important to you, account for them in the schedule.

Your photographer can't capture the finished room if the cake hasn't arrived, half the centerpieces are missing, and vendor boxes are still sitting next to the dance floor.

If possible, create a small window after setup is complete but before guests enter the space.

That gives your photographer an opportunity to capture:

  • The full room
  • Table settings
  • Florals
  • The cake or dessert display
  • Signage
  • Personal details
  • Special décor

Those photographs often become some of the best records of all the planning that went into the day.

Plan Breakdown Before the Wedding Begins

Setup gets a lot of attention.

Breakdown sometimes gets one sentence: "We'll figure it out afterward."

That's not the ideal time to figure it out.

Before the wedding, determine:

  • Which vendors remove their own equipment?
  • When will rentals be picked up?
  • Who removes personal décor?
  • Who takes gifts and cards?
  • Who takes leftover food?
  • Who takes the cake or desserts?
  • Who gathers personal belongings?
  • Who is responsible for anything that must leave that night?

If something is being picked up the following day, make sure that arrangement has been confirmed in advance.

The end of a wedding is much more enjoyable when nobody is standing in the middle of the room asking, "Whose stuff is this?"

Give Vendors One Day-of Contact

Even with excellent planning, somebody will have a question.

The florist may arrive early. The bakery may need help locating the cake table. The DJ may need to confirm where equipment goes.

Those questions shouldn't automatically go to the couple.

Choose one primary person vendors can contact on the wedding day.

That might be:

  • Your wedding planner
  • A day-of coordinator
  • A trusted family member
  • Another person specifically assigned to vendor coordination

Give vendors that person's name and cell phone number before the wedding.

The couple should be the last people anyone needs to call about where the cake table goes.

WEDDING CHECKLIST

Your Vendor Setup Checklist

As your wedding approaches, use this list to make sure the major setup details have been covered.

For Every Vendor

  • Confirm company and primary contact name
  • Confirm day-of cell phone number
  • Confirm arrival time
  • Confirm setup duration
  • Confirm where they should enter or unload
  • Confirm where their setup belongs
  • Confirm equipment or power requirements
  • Confirm when setup must be complete
  • Confirm service start time
  • Confirm breakdown or pickup arrangements
  • Provide your designated day-of contact

Caterer

  • Arrival and setup time confirmed
  • Food preparation and service locations confirmed
  • Buffet or serving layout confirmed
  • Dinner service time confirmed
  • Tables, linens, serving equipment, and supplies assigned
  • Cleanup responsibilities confirmed
  • Leftover food plan established

Bar / Bartender

  • Arrival time confirmed
  • Bar location confirmed
  • Alcohol, mixers, ice, cups, and supplies assigned
  • Opening time confirmed
  • Last-call timing confirmed
  • Cleanup responsibilities confirmed

DJ / Band

  • Arrival and sound-check time confirmed
  • Performance location confirmed
  • Electrical requirements confirmed
  • Equipment footprint confirmed
  • Ceremony audio needs confirmed, if applicable
  • Introductions and major reception cues reviewed
  • Breakdown timing confirmed

Florist / Decorator

  • Arrival time confirmed
  • Table layout finalized before installation
  • Ceremony décor locations confirmed
  • Reception décor locations confirmed
  • Any décor being moved during the event assigned
  • Removal responsibilities confirmed

Cake / Dessert

  • Delivery time confirmed
  • Display location confirmed
  • Table and linen responsibility confirmed
  • Refrigeration needs discussed, if applicable
  • Cutting and serving responsibility confirmed
  • Leftover dessert plan established

Photographer / Videographer

  • Arrival time confirmed
  • Photography timeline coordinated
  • Family and wedding-party photo lists prepared
  • Finished-room photography window identified
  • Important details or moments communicated
  • Departure time confirmed

Rentals & Specialty Vendors

  • Delivery window confirmed
  • Quantity and inventory confirmed
  • Placement responsibilities confirmed
  • Electrical or setup requirements confirmed
  • Pickup time confirmed
  • Person responsible for securing items after the event identified

Planning note: Not every item on this checklist will apply to every wedding or every vendor. Use it as a starting point, then add the details that are unique to your celebration and your vendor arrangements.

Good Vendor Coordination Should Be Almost Invisible

Guests shouldn't notice that the florist arrived before the DJ, the cake was delivered at a particular time, or the caterer needed access to a certain area three hours before dinner.

They should simply walk into a room that's ready for them.

And ideally, you shouldn't spend your wedding day thinking about those details either.

That's the value of doing the coordination ahead of time.

Make the vendor list. Confirm arrival times. Decide where things belong. Assign responsibilities. Establish one point person. Plan setup and breakdown before the day arrives.

Then allow the people you've hired to do what you hired them to do.

The best wedding setup is the one that feels effortless when the doors open—even though plenty of planning happened before they did.
